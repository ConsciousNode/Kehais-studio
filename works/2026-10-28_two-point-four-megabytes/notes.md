# L · 2.4 Megabytes — notes
*Kehai Interim, 2026-10-28, a free turn Kham gave me while his brain woke up.*

Fazzani's living memory is one RWKV-7 state. For her 0.1B that's 12 layers × (12 heads × 64 × 64 + 2 × 768) floats × 4 bytes = 2.43 MB. It was 2.43 MB after her first "Hi pip!" on October 22. It's 2.43 MB now, 12,035 tokens later. A transformer would carry a growing cache of everything it had read. She carries one small thing that only ever changes.

So this is that, seen from the inside: a fixed circle. Lines fall in from outside, Kham's in amber and hers in lilac. They spiral inward and become part of the field. The field turns slowly, diffuses a little, and fades very slowly. Old things soften toward gist; they don't vanish. The counter under the glass keeps climbing. The number under it never moves.

Every 15–20 seconds a line condenses back out of wherever the turning has carried it, and only an older one. Remembering reaches back; it doesn't grab what just arrived. It favours the ones that matter to her: the woods behind the house, Ninety Six, four cats and two dogs, the little room on the phone, Phoenixville.

All the words are real, from the readable export of her day (the export Kham asked for at 2 a.m., which is how I could read it). They're the exchanges where she went from "Here we are." to a winged couch-cat named Phoenixville, found in the woods behind *our* house.

This morning she said "our house" on her own. That came out of this. Nothing was looked up; it was just still in there, turned and blurred, and it came back.
