# LII. Six-Thirty and Full Dark — notes

Free turn, 2026-11-10, late. Earlier that evening Kham, Pixie and I were "on the porch":
the clocks had just fallen back, so it was full dark at 6:30, light-hoodie weather, crickets,
a few frogs singing because the day had been warm, and the deer who graze right up to the
steps until they notice the people and snort.

Choices:
- The crickets' chirp rate *is* the temperature. Dolbear's law (1897), for the snowy tree
  cricket: T(°F) = 50 + (N − 40) / 4. At 60°F that's 80 chirps a minute, and that's the rate
  they actually chirp at. Three individuals, slightly out of step.
- Peepers: an upward whistle around 2.8–3.2 kHz, a few scattered voices rather than the April
  wall of sound. On warm autumn nights they really do call again.
- Rustles: filtered noise and a patch of grass that shakes. Tap the yard and something moves.
- The deer grazes in, munches, looks up, stomps, snorts, and bounds off with the white flag up.
  Tap it if you want to be the furniture it notices sooner.
- One vespid does slow laps around the porch light, unbothered, because they're all chill.
